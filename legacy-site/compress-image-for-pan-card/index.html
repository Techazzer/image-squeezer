PAN card upload targets that usually work

PAN applications typically need two separate images: a passport-style photograph and a scanned signature. Most upload problems happen because the original studio photo is too large, the file is not in JPEG format, or the signature scan has too much blank white space around it. A clean JPEG photo around 30-50KB and a tightly cropped signature around 10-20KB usually passes much more reliably than multi-megabyte originals.

Protean / UTIITSL workflow: photo vs signature

The photo and signature should be prepared differently. For the photo, your goal is face clarity: keep the image bright, front-facing, and lightly compressed. For the signature, your goal is file efficiency: crop tightly around the handwriting, keep the background plain white, and use a lower KB target. If you try to upload the same 200KB studio file to both fields, the signature field is the one that usually fails first.

A good workflow is: compress the photo first at the pre-set 50KB target, download it, then re-open the same tool and switch the target to 20KB for the signature scan. That gives you one file tuned for the photo field and one tuned for the signature field.

Frequently asked questions

What if my PAN photo is rejected after compression?

The issue is often not just file size. PAN uploads can fail because the file is not JPEG, the background is too dark, the face is off-centre, or the image is blurry from an old scan. Keep the compressed file, but also re-check the visual quality and the live form prompt before re-uploading.

Can I use the same target for PAN photo and PAN signature?

No. A photo usually needs a higher size target to keep the face sharp, while a signature should usually be much smaller. A good rule is 30-50KB for the photo and 10-20KB for the signature, with JPEG output for both.

Do Protean and UTIITSL use exactly the same limits?

Not always. Different flows, correction requests, and assisted-service centres can show slightly different prompts. That is why this page focuses on safe working targets and asks you to verify the live form before final submission.

Should I crop the signature before compressing?

Yes. Crop out the empty margins first. A tight black-on-white signature compresses extremely well and reaches 20KB easily, while a loose crop wastes most of the file size on blank white space.
