Why compress WebP images?

WebP is already Google's efficient image format, but a high-resolution WebP from a camera or design tool can still be 1–5MB. For web publishing, social media, or email, keeping WebP under 150–200KB ensures fast load times without visible quality loss. This tool compresses WebP files directly — or converts JPEG/PNG to optimised WebP.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this tool upload my WebP to a server?

No. Your image is compressed using the browser's Canvas API — it never leaves your device. Unlike TinyPNG, Squoosh's server mode, or other cloud compressors, all processing is local. This is important for sensitive images (product photos before launch, personal photos, ID documents).

Can I convert JPEG/PNG to WebP and compress at the same time?

Yes. Upload any JPEG or PNG, select WebP as the output format, set your target quality or size, and click Compress. You get a compressed WebP output in one step — useful for converting a batch of product images or blog photos to WebP format.

What quality setting should I use for WebP?

85% WebP is the standard for web publishing — it's visually identical to the original at normal viewing sizes and achieves excellent compression. For thumbnails or icons, you can go as low as 75%. For print-quality preservation, use 90–95%.
