How to plan for a specific maturity target (3 steps)

  1. Define your target corpus — how much money do you need at the end? For retirement: estimate your monthly expenses × 300 (25-year corpus at 4% withdrawal rate). For education: research current fees and apply 10% annual inflation for the years until your child enters college.

  2. Enter your timeline — how many years until you need the money? Then try different monthly SIP amounts in the calculator until the maturity value meets your target. The calculator instantly shows which monthly amount hits your goal.

  3. Start immediately. Every month of delay on a long-term SIP costs significantly more than it appears. A 30-year-old starting a ₹10,000 SIP at 12% reaches ₹3.53Cr by 60. Starting at 35 only gives ₹1.89Cr by 60 — ₹1.64Cr less, for just 5 years of delay.

Frequently asked questions

What is SIP maturity value?

SIP maturity value is the total amount you receive when your SIP tenure ends. It includes everything: your invested principal (monthly amount × months) plus all the compounded returns earned over the period. This is the number that tells you whether your SIP met your financial goal.

Can I withdraw SIP before maturity?

Yes — open-ended mutual funds allow withdrawal anytime (except ELSS which has a 3-year lock-in per installment). Withdrawing early means you receive the current NAV value of your units, which may be higher or lower than planned depending on market conditions at withdrawal time.

How much corpus is enough for retirement in India?

A common rule: multiply your expected monthly retirement expenses by 300 (25x annual expenses, supporting a 4% annual withdrawal). If you expect ₹50,000/month in expenses (in today's terms), target ₹1.5 crore (inflation-adjusted).
