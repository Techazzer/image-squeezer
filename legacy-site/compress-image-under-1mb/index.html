How much quality do you lose going to 1MB?

A typical 12-megapixel smartphone JPEG at full quality is about 4–6MB. Compressing it to 900KB (under 1MB) involves a quality reduction from ~95% to roughly 75–80% JPEG quality. On screen — phone, laptop, or desktop — this difference is invisible. You would only detect it by zooming in past 150% and comparing side by side. For sharing, uploading, emailing, or social media, under-1MB quality is perfectly fine.

The exception: if you need to print at A4 or larger, keep the original. For anything digital, under 1MB is sufficient. Our tool shows you a before/after preview so you can verify quality before downloading.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why 900KB instead of exactly 1MB?

File size calculations differ slightly between tools, browsers, and operating systems. A file that shows as 1.0MB in your file browser might be technically 1,024KB or 1,048KB depending on how MB is calculated. Using 900KB as the target ensures you're safely under 1MB by any measurement system — preventing frustrating upload errors at the last moment.

Can I compress a PNG to under 1MB?

Yes. Upload your PNG and set the target to 900KB. If the PNG has transparency that you want to keep, the tool can output PNG under 1MB. If transparency isn't needed, switch the format to JPEG — you'll get a much smaller file at the same visual quality (JPEG is typically 5–10x smaller than PNG for photos).

My image is already 1.2MB — why can't I just crop it?

Cropping reduces the image dimensions, which also reduces file size, but at the cost of losing content on the edges. Compression keeps the full image dimensions while reducing file size by adjusting quality. For images where you want to keep the full frame (portraits, documents, group photos), compression is always better than cropping.
